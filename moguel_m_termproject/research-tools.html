Research support

Use AI to explore information, not replace reliable sources

AI tools can help you understand unfamiliar topics, generate search terms, organize questions, and summarize information. However, AI responses are not automatically accurate and should never be treated as verified evidence.

Useful starting points

Ways AI can support research

AI is most helpful at the beginning of the research process, when you are developing questions and deciding what information to find.

Understand a topic

Ask AI for a simple explanation of an unfamiliar concept before searching for academic sources.

Example prompt: Explain climate justice in simple terms for a first-year college student.

Develop research questions

Use AI to turn a broad topic into focused questions that are easier to investigate.

Example prompt: Give me five focused research questions about food insecurity among college students.

Generate search terms

Ask for related keywords, synonyms, or phrases that may improve searches in a library database.

Example prompt: Suggest academic search terms related to remote learning and student engagement.

Organize findings

AI can help group notes into themes after you have collected information from reliable sources.

Example prompt: Organize these research notes into three major themes without adding new facts.

Verification process

Check every important claim

  1. Identify the factual claim made by the AI tool.
  2. Search for the information in your college library databases.
  3. Look for a reliable source that directly supports the claim.
  4. Check the author, organization, publication date, and evidence.
  5. Compare the information with at least one additional source.
  6. Cite the original source, not the AI response.

AI can invent sources

An AI tool may produce a book title, article, author, quotation, or web address that appears real but does not exist.

Never include a citation in an assignment unless you have located and reviewed the original source yourself.

Source evaluation

Questions to ask before using a source

Source evaluation checklist
Area to evaluate Questions to ask Warning signs
Authority Who created the information, and what qualifications do they have? No author, unclear organization, or unsupported expertise.
Accuracy Does the source provide evidence, references, or verifiable data? Claims are presented without evidence or contain obvious errors.
Currency When was the information published or last updated? The information is outdated for a topic that changes quickly.
Purpose Is the source trying to inform, persuade, sell, or entertain? The language is extremely emotional, biased, or promotional.
Relevance Does the source directly answer your research question? The source only mentions the topic briefly or lacks useful detail.

Best practice

Start with your college library, academic databases, government websites, recognized educational institutions, and reputable professional organizations.

Before submitting

Research accuracy checklist

  • I located the original source for every quotation and citation.
  • I confirmed that each source actually exists.
  • I checked the author and publication date.
  • I compared important claims with more than one reliable source.
  • I used the citation style required by my instructor.
  • I did not cite an AI chatbot as though it were an academic source.
  • I followed my instructor’s rules for disclosing AI assistance.
